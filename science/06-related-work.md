# Related work and contribution

[Scientific overview](README.md) · [Bibliography](../references/README.md)

The contribution is the matched quantum communication law for a fixed-axis random squeezing channel under the **physical oscillator energy constraint**:

```math
Q_\sigma(E)=\log_2\!\ln E+O_\sigma(1),
\qquad E\longrightarrow\infty,
\qquad \sigma>0\ \text{fixed}.
```

The upper bound allows arbitrary correlated inputs across channel uses and collective decoding. The lower bound uses finite-energy, non-Gaussian subspaces and the established coherent-information coding theorem. The noise model, logarithmic-coordinate reduction, general entropy inequalities and sign/parity logical subsystem have antecedents. Their relationship to the capacity theorem is set out below; the bibliography gives the primary sources and stable source versions.

## Random squeezing and logarithmic coordinates

Sakuldee and Tonekaboni [ST24] study random mixtures of continuous-variable unitary errors generated by linear and quadratic oscillator operators. Their Section IV includes real squeezing noise. In equations (70)–(72), changing the Wigner variables to logarithms of the absolute quadratures converts the multiplicative action into a convolution. They then use intermediate control operations to filter the noise. A rotation by a quarter turn reverses a squeeze, allowing cancellation for a constant disturbance along the path and suppression in a fast-intervention regime.

Those observations establish the relevant noise model and coordinate mechanism. Here, each complete channel use instead has one independent, unobserved squeeze draw; there are no interventions inside a use. The question is the best asymptotic quantum rate at a specified mean input photon number. The new step is to connect the logarithmic representation to that physical energy and prove matching bounds for unrestricted codes. The coordinate change alone does not give this result.

## The protected qubit is inherited

Marshall, James, Paler and Lau [MJPL19, Section III, equation (18)] introduce quadrature-sign parity encoding. Their logical Pauli observables include the sign of a quadrature and oscillator parity. Both commute with every squeeze about the same axis. Consequently, exact preservation of this logical qubit by any mixture of those squeezes follows directly from their logical subsystem.

The sign factor in the logarithmic representation is this same qubit, with a possible relabeling of the logical Pauli axes. Its finite-energy realization is a useful explicit example, and it makes positive transmission transparent even at large noise strength. The existence of the protected subsystem is not claimed as a new encoding principle. Lau and Clerk [LC19, Methods] also use the encoding to analyze noise tolerance in bosonic transduction; that is a different channel and performance problem.

## Compact phase dephasing has a different energy frontier

Arqand, Memarzadeh and Mancini [AMM20] analyze number-generated Gaussian phase dephasing and derive a single-letter quantum-capacity optimization, with a finite high-energy limit. Lami and Wilde [LW23] subsequently determine the unconstrained quantum, private and corresponding two-way assisted capacities for arbitrary bosonic phase-dephasing laws: their expression is the relative entropy of the phase distribution to the uniform law.

The difference is operational, rather than a replacement of one distribution by another. Number rotations form a compact group, and the number generator has a discrete spectrum. The squeeze generator has a continuous spectrum with multiplicity two, and its relation to oscillator energy is different. A smooth, fixed phase-noise law can therefore give a finite capacity ceiling, while the squeeze channel considered here has an unbounded, double-logarithmic energy dependence. Generalized dephasing and the coherent-information framework are shared ingredients; the cited phase-channel formulas do not imply the squeeze-energy law.

## Why Gaussian canonical equivalence does not settle the result

Holevo's canonical classification [H07] concerns one-mode Gaussian channels up to canonical unitary transformations. The squeezing channel here is a mixture of Gaussian unitaries, but the mixture is not a Gaussian channel. For example, a vacuum input gives

```math
\langle Q^2\rangle=\frac12e^{2\sigma^2},
\qquad
\langle Q^4\rangle=\frac34e^{8\sigma^2},
```

which violates the centered-Gaussian relation between the second and fourth moments for every positive noise width.

The exact logarithmic unitary does reduce the action to one-quadrature translation on a continuous factor, together with the noiseless sign factor. It also transforms the oscillator Hamiltonian into a quadratic form with exponential coefficients. Applying a usual translation-channel capacity formula while retaining its usual quadratic energy observable would therefore change the task. The proof must keep the transformed physical energy throughout. Gaussian optimality is neither assumed nor needed for the stated capacity law.

## Classical fading: an important analogy, not a quantum proof

Lapidoth and Moser [LM03] establish double-logarithmic high-signal-to-noise capacity behavior for broad classes of classical noncoherent fading channels, using a dual capacity bound and suitable logarithmic-amplitude input distributions. Thus neither the double-logarithmic function nor its association with uncertain multiplicative transformations is new by itself.

The present channel acts on quantum amplitudes by a reversible phase-space dilation before the unknown draw is averaged. The theorem concerns preservation of entanglement under arbitrary quantum encoders and collective decoding. A classical fading result does not establish its coherent-information lower bound, the quantum converse or the photon cost of its quantum subspaces. The common logarithmic geometry provides intuition; the tasks and converses remain distinct.

## Entropy and coding ingredients

The main converse uses Gibbs variational bounds, relative-entropy data processing, the oscillator Mehler kernel and the maximum-entropy property of a thermal oscillator. These are established tools. The problem-specific steps are the environment's fixed-resolution generator measurement, the harmonic heat-trace mass in each generator bin, and the matching finite-energy construction. Frank and Lieb's state-dependent Fourier entropic uncertainty relation [FL12, Theorem 2.1 and its Fourier-convention remark] supplies a related alternative entropy route; it is not required by the spectral-bin proof given here.

Wilde and Qi [WQ18] supply the energy-constrained coding theorem. Their Definition 3, Condition 1 and Theorem 5 identify the Gibbs and finite-output-entropy assumptions under which regularized coherent information equals the average-energy entanglement-transmission capacity. The oscillator has a finite partition function at every positive inverse temperature. For the present centered Gaussian squeeze law, the output harmonic energy is the input harmonic energy multiplied by a finite factor depending only on the noise width, ensuring a finite output-entropy bound at every finite input budget. This verifies the relevant coding hypotheses; it is not a new coding theorem or a finite-block implementation claim.

## The Gaussian-input comparison

The additional coherent-information bound applies to every correlated multimode Gaussian physical input and is uniform in its energy. Its radial translation-channel step uses degradability and Gaussian extremality at fixed covariance, consistent with the established Gaussian-channel machinery of Wolf, Pérez-García and Giedke [WPGG07]. The classical log-radius calculation uses the noncentral-chi-square Poisson mixture and log moments recorded by Pav [P15], together with standard digamma recurrences. The account proves the required monotonicity directly and makes no priority claim for that classical lemma.

Combining those ingredients with the physical logarithmic coordinate bounds Gaussian-input coherent information per use by a constant depending on the noise width. The unrestricted non-Gaussian construction instead diverges. This is an input-state coherent-information comparison, not a no-go theorem for every apparatus containing Gaussian operations.

## Related public capacity accounts

These comparisons use the pinned public accounts listed in the [bibliography](../references/README.md#related-public-accounts).

| Account | Shared foundation | Different task and result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bosonic Phase Noise Capacity | Generalized dephasing, coherent information and control of the full mean-energy tail | Compact number rotations; finite-resolution angular entropy determines capacity within a bounded additive error. Its phase histogram is not the squeeze generator's energy geometry. |
| Bosonic Noise Dimension | Unobserved random unitary noise, arbitrary-code energy converses and resolution-dependent entropy | Weyl displacements, including noncommuting planar errors. Its information-dimension law is explicitly restricted to phase-space kicks and does not determine random squeezing capacity. |

These accounts share an interest in how physical energy resolves noise. Their theorems do not claim a universal rule covering every one-parameter oscillator transformation. The present theorem adds the noncompact hyperbolic case with its different rate law; it does not contradict their stated models or count their results as new results here.

## Scope of the conclusion

The model keeps the squeeze axis known and fixed, draws the noise independently for each use and charges unconditional mean input photons. The noise width is fixed before taking the high-energy limit. There is no photon loss, displacement noise, axis jitter or revealed noise record. No uniform zero-noise limit, exact finite-energy capacity, efficient encoder, finite-block performance or practical throughput advantage is established. These restrictions belong with the capacity statement, including when it is compared with other oscillator channels.
